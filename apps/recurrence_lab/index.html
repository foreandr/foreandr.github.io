Why Some Recurrences Stay Clean And Others Explode

This app is not just about plotting values. It is also about how much past information each recurrence has to carry forward.

Good + Painleve

The Rigid Track

These recurrences lock the future to the past with a fixed and manageable rule.

What that means: to compute a far-away term like a_100, you usually only need one or two recent terms such as a_99 and a_98.

What you see in the graph: even when the values grow very large, they usually grow in a smooth and structured way. The pattern does not suddenly "forget" its shape.

Why Painleve still fits here: even when the recurrence is nonlinear, the interactions stay organized by hidden symmetry. The information does not tangle into chaos, so the system stays integrable.

Bad / Chaos

The Information Explosion

These recurrences do not depend on just the latest step. They can depend on the entire history of the sequence.

What that means: to compute something like a_100, the rule may need to combine many earlier terms at once: a_0, a_1, ..., a_99.

What you see in the graph: the later terms contain more compressed history than the early ones. The sequence effectively remembers and re-mixes its own past, which is why complexity and chaos appear.

Big intuition: in the rigid track, the future is a short continuation of the recent past. In the bad track, the future is a crowded summary of almost everything that came before.
