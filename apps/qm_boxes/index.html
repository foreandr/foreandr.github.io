Magic boxes

A toy model of the lecture idea: no phenomenon until it is an observed phenomenon (Wheeler). Buttons are questions; the light is the only answer you get. Two-box and many-copy setups behave differently — the same “common sense” about hidden cards cannot fit both.

    This is a pedagogical simulation, not a quantum device. It encodes the story Feynman tells: with two boxes you can arrange perfect agreement on three questions; with three (or more) copies correlated on “the left button” only, you cannot also demand perfect agreement on all questions — the counting argument (pairs that match) clashes with ¼ vs ¾ statistics. Bell’s theorem sharpens that clash mathematically; here you only meet the flavor.
