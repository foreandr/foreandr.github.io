NORMALIZED COMPRESSION DISTANCE

The NCD approximates the Normalized Information Distance — the length of the shortest program that transforms x into y, relative to the longer of the two. NCD ≈ 0 means the strings share nearly all their algorithmic content. NCD ≈ 1 means they are maximally different. Works on DNA, music, code, text, languages — without any domain knowledge.

Bennett et al. 1998 (Information Distance) · Cilibrasi & Vitányi 2005 (Clustering by Compression) · Gács 1974 (Symmetry of AIT)
NCD(x,y) = [ C(xy) − min(C(x), C(y)) ] / max(C(x), C(y))

LOGICAL DEPTH ESTIMATOR

Bennett (1988) distinguished two types of "non-random" strings: shallow structured (easily compressed AND generated, like "AAAA…"), and logically deep (compressed/structured but requiring massive computation to produce, like π or Mandelbrot escape times). This module places your string on the Depth Map alongside canonical examples.

Bennett 1988 (Logical Depth) · Antunes et al. 2006 (Computational Depth) · Levin 1974 (Information Conservation)

CHAITIN'S Ω — THE HALTING PROBABILITY

Ω is the probability that a randomly chosen program halts. It is a well-defined real number between 0 and 1 — yet it is provably uncomputable. Knowing k bits of Ω lets you decide the Halting Problem for all programs of length ≤ k. This simulation enumerates all 2-state 2-symbol Turing Machines (12⁴ = 20,736 total) and tracks the empirical halting fraction.

Chaitin 1975 (Ω definition) · Chaitin 1987 (AIT book) · Rado 1962 (Busy Beaver) · Soler-Toscano & Zenil 2014
Ω = Σp: U(p) halts 2−|p|  ·  Ω is Σ1-complete — unknowable, uncompressible, but real

ENTROPY BITMAP VISUALIZER

Render a string as a 2D pixel grid. In Entropy mode, each pixel is colored by the local Shannon entropy of its neighborhood — blue = low entropy (structured), red = high entropy (random). This makes the "texture" of information directly visible. Structure emerges as islands of order inside chaos.

Martin-Löf 1966 · Shannon 1948 · Zenil 2011 (Computable Algorithmic Probability) · V'yugin 1987 (Defect of Randomness)
